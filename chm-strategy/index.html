Section 2 — Organizational Profile

About the Cooperativa

CHM manages distributed energy resources across four municipalities in Puerto Rico's Cordillera Central, operating hydroelectric, solar PV, and battery storage systems through three flagship projects.

💧
Hidroenergía Renace
Rehabilitation of Caonillas-1 (20 MW), Caonillas-2 (4 MW), and Dos Bocas (15 MW) hydroelectric plants. Combined generation: ~81K MWh/yr.
☀️
ReEnFoCo
Community solar PV + BESS at municipal substations in Adjuntas, Jayuya, Lares, and Utuado. SMA, Enphase, Fortress Power equipment.
🔌
Microrred de la Montaña
First inter-municipal microgrid in Puerto Rico — 38 kV sub-transmission linking all plants, SCADA/EMS dispatch, islanding capability.
🏛️
Customer Management
Microsoft Dataverse cloud platform, DOE/USDA federal partnerships, member PII protected under Puerto Rico privacy regulations.

Section 3 — Threat Analysis

Threat Landscape

CHM faces four primary threat categories, each mapped to STRIDE, NERC CIP mitigating controls, and specific CHM assets. The 2026 NERC CIP Roadmap identified supply chain attacks, physical attacks, phishing, and ransomware as the top four BPS risks.

🌀
Natural & Environmental
Category 4–5 hurricanes; STRIDE: Denial of Service at physical layer. Hurricane Maria 2017 left parts of the island without power for nearly a year.
🌐
Nation-State APTs
Volt Typhoon targets low-impact BES via weak VPN controls. Salt Typhoon intercepts unencrypted DNP3/Modbus over carrier telecom links.
💻
Cybercriminal
Ransomware ranked #5 of 22 BPS risks with likelihood score 4.25/5.0. Phishing into billing systems can pivot to OT turbine controls.
👥
Insider Threats
Ranked #4 in NERC 2026 survey with impact score 4.67/5.0 — joint-highest. Spans intentional, negligent, and accidental categories.

Section 6 — NIST CSF 2.0

Cybersecurity Strategy by Function

NIST CSF 2.0 released February 26, 2024 adds a sixth function — Govern — to the original five. All controls are organized into Administrative, Physical, and Technical/Logical categories. Every control traces back to a NERC CIP requirement.

GV
Govern
Establishes organizational context and accountability. Added in CSF 2.0 — governance failures are the most common root cause of security program breakdowns.
  • Board-approved cybersecurity risk strategy reviewed annually
  • Cybersecurity Program Lead reports to executive director
  • Three foundational documents: Policy, Risk Appetite Statement, Program Plan
  • NERC CIP-003 senior management accountability requirement
  • Quarterly board metrics: policy review rate, open exceptions, vendor contract coverage
  • Read-only, tamper-evident document management system
ID
Identify
Gives CHM a clear picture of the cyber-physical environment. Protective resources flow to the highest-risk assets first.
  • Comprehensive asset register — updated within 30 days of any change
  • NERC CIP-002 BCS categorization for all equipment
  • 2.9 MW critical load mapping: food, medical, water, emergency services
  • Quarterly physical walkthroughs to verify asset register accuracy
  • Monthly automated network discovery vs. approved baseline
  • Rogue device alert to Program Lead within 24 hours
PR
Protect
Safeguards that reduce likelihood and impact of a cybersecurity event: IAM, data security, network architecture, and awareness training.
  • Role-based access control (RBAC) — 4 roles: read-only, plant operator, sys admin, sec admin
  • MFA on all remote BCS access — #1 impact control per NERC 2026
  • CIP-007 patch management: critical ≤35 days, high ≤90 days
  • 3-2-1 backup rule; golden-image offline backups in fireproof safes
  • Purdue Model NGFW with DPI; all inter-zone traffic denied by default
  • TLS 1.3 tunnels over all carrier-provided links (DNP3, Modbus)
  • WPA3-AES on all TP-Link Wi-Fi; DIGI TrustFence secure boot + signed firmware
  • Annual CIP-004 security training + 2x/yr phishing simulations
DE
Detect
Continuous monitoring to identify anomalous events before they escalate. 24-hour detection across all unmanned generation sites.
  • SIEM aggregating logs from firewalls, SCADA, RTUs, auth systems
  • CIP-015 Internal Network Security Monitoring (INSM) within ESPs
  • Anomaly detection: failed logins, off-hours config changes, unusual SCADA volumes
  • Detection rules updated quarterly; 90-day log retention
  • CCTV at all High Security Zone entry points; tamper-evident seals
  • Third-party SOC for after-hours 24/7 monitoring
RS
Respond
Structured incident response capability meeting NERC CIP-008 requirements. Rapid escalation to E-ISAC, NERC, and CISA.
  • CSIRP with designated Incident Response Team (IRT) — 5 roles
  • E-ISAC notification within 1 hour; NERC formal report within 24 hours
  • SIEM auto-tickets for High+ severity events; IRT acknowledgment ≤15 min
  • SCADA Triage Checklist: verify → sever iDMZ link → switch to manual control
  • NAC auto-quarantine of endpoints failing posture assessment
  • 2x/yr tabletop exercises; exercise findings incorporated within 30 days
RC
Recover
Prioritized recovery mapped to community lifeline services. RTOs ensure grid-critical systems are restored before administrative systems.
  • Priority 1: EMS (RTO 4 hr) → Priority 2: Hydro plant controls (RTO 8 hr)
  • Priority 3: BESS/Solar inverters → Priority 4: Billing & customer systems
  • Golden-image backups on encrypted offline USB; hash-verified monthly
  • Pre-positioned spare DIGI IX10, TP-Link hardware at each plant site
  • 8-hour battery backup for control room equipment during islanding events
  • CIP-009: documented, tested recovery plans reviewed every 15 calendar months
Section 7 — Regulatory Framework

NERC CIP Standards Integration

13 active NERC CIP standards form the technical control backbone. CHM's combined Caonillas-Dos Bocas system at 39 MW connected to the 38 kV network qualifies as Low Impact BES Cyber Systems under CIP-002.

Framework Integration: NIST CSF 2.0 serves as the governance architecture; NERC CIP provides the prescriptive technical control library. Using both gives CHM an approach that satisfies regulatory auditors and communicates security posture clearly to community leaders and funding agencies.
CIP-002
Asset Categorization
CHM categorizes the combined Caonillas-Dos Bocas system as Low Impact BCS under Criterion 2.6. Initial categorization within 30 days of operational control; annual review.
CIP-003
Security Management Controls
Documents electronic access, physical security, monitoring, and incident response for low-impact BCS. CIP-003-11 (proposed) adds MFA + malicious communication detection.
CIP-004
Personnel and Training
Background checks, drug screening, and employment verification for all OT-adjacent roles. Annual security training before access granted and each calendar year thereafter.
CIP-005
Electronic Security Perimeters
Defines ESP around BES Cyber Systems. All External Routable Connectivity identified and controlled. MFA required for all interactive remote access.
CIP-006
Physical Security
Badge + PIN at Caonillas and Dos Bocas High Security Zone entry points. CCTV, motion lighting, tamper-evident seals, cabinet locks on all DIN-rail enclosures.
CIP-007
System Security Management
Ports/services minimized per function. 35-day patch review cycle. SIEM event monitoring; 90-day log retention. All unauthorized ports disabled and sealed.
CIP-008
Incident Reporting
CSIRP satisfies CIP-008. E-ISAC notification ≤1 hour; NERC formal report ≤24 hours. IRT documented with 5 designated roles. Tested via tabletop exercises 2x/yr.
CIP-009
Recovery Plans
Documented RTOs: EMS (4 hr), hydro controls (8 hr). Recovery plans tested every 15 calendar months. Lessons-learned reviews within 2 weeks of any incident.
CIP-010
Configuration Management
Baseline configuration docs for all BES Cyber Assets. Unauthorized changes detected within 35 days. Automated tools generate and compare baselines every monitoring cycle.
CIP-011
Information Protection
BES Cyber System Information protected in storage, transit, and disposal. AES-256 encryption. Physical media destroyed per CIP-011 at decommission.
CIP-012
Communications Security
Protects real-time monitoring and control data between control center and LUMA Energy dispatch. TLS 1.3 tunnels over all carrier-provided fiber, copper, and cellular links.
CIP-013
Supply Chain Risk Management
Mandatory for ICS hardware, software, and services vendors. SBOM required for all BCS software. Vendors must notify CHM within 24 hours of breach or vulnerability discovery.
CIP-015 ★
Internal Network Security Monitoring
Adopted May 9, 2024. INSM within ESPs of applicable high/medium BCS. Passive network taps feed packet capture to SIEM. Directly counters Volt Typhoon lateral movement.
Section 8 — Supply Chain Risk Management

Supply Chain Risk Program

NERC 2026 ranked supply chain attacks #1 of 22 BPS risks with the highest composite weighted score of 10.3. CHM's Microrred depends on dozens of vendors across inverters, routers, BESS, and analytics platforms.

Vendor Lifecycle Controls
1
Selection
Mandatory cybersecurity questionnaire: certifications, vulnerability disclosure, patch support timelines. Vendors without foundational controls are ineligible for BCS-adjacent contracts.
2
Contract Requirements
4 mandatory CIP-013 provisions: 24-hr vulnerability notification, 24-hr breach notification, SBOM delivery, and remote access termination rights.
3
SBOM & CVE Scanning
SBOM documents every library, OSS package, and module. Automated CVE scanning identifies affected components within hours of CVE publication — not weeks.
4
Ongoing Monitoring
Annual questionnaire reviews. Vendor breach activates vendor CSIRP: isolate access → assess exposure → notify regulators → update risk register within 5 business days.

Section 9 — Implementation

36-Month Implementation Roadmap

Three phases aligned with Microrred de la Montaña operational stages: ReEnFoCo solar/storage, Hidroenergia Renace hydro rehabilitation, and full microgrid integration. Each phase advances CHM one NIST CSF tier.

01
Phase 1 · Foundation
Governance & Identification
Months 1–12
Tier 1 → Tier 2 (Risk Informed)
  • Appoint Cybersecurity Program Lead; create Cybersecurity Policy, Risk Appetite Statement, and Program Plan
  • Complete initial BES Cyber Asset inventory and CIP-002 categorization (Table 5-1)
  • Implement MFA + least-privilege RBAC on all BCS-adjacent systems including SCADA and DIGI IX10 gateways
  • Deploy SIEM with baseline correlation rules; integrate all existing log sources
  • Conduct first CIP-004 security awareness training + first phishing simulation
  • Complete CSIRP; assign IRT roles; run first tabletop (hurricane comms-loss scenario)
  • Complete vendor assessments for SMA, Enphase, Fortress Power, DIGI, XENDEE
02
Phase 2 · Hardening
OT Network Security
Months 13–24
Tier 2 → Tier 3 (Repeatable)
  • Deploy industrial-grade NGFWs at each plant site; configure Purdue Model segmentation
  • Activate INSM sensors (CIP-015) within OT process control network zones
  • Expand SIEM log sources from plant-level systems; begin behavioral baselines
  • Formalize CIP-007 patch management; activate CIP-010 automated config monitoring
  • Encrypt all SCADA data over 38 kV links via TLS 1.3 tunnels (CIP-012)
  • Hire/contract OT security specialist for OT network monitoring and maintenance
  • CIP-003-11 controls live before 38 kV interconnection energizes
03
Phase 3 · Optimization
Unified Security Operations
Months 25–36
Sustain → Tier 3 (Validated)
  • Third-party SOC contract activated for 24-hour monitoring at all sites
  • Automated CVE scanning vs. SBOM inventory; weekly patch prioritization reports
  • First NERC CIP compliance self-assessment; preparation for Regional Entity audit
  • Annual cybersecurity risk assessment comparing prior-year risk profile
  • Updated Target Organizational Profile with new 3-year security targets
  • Validated Tier 3 positions CHM for DOE CESER and USDA rural cooperative cybersecurity grant funding
